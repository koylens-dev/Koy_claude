# Study format

Title: `# Practical Study: <Reference>, *<Short evocative title>*`
Then a one-line **big idea** in bold. The big idea should be one sentence that could stand on a poster, and it should point to Jesus: what He has done or who He is, and how that shapes us. For example: "God sowed His only Son generously. Every open hand begins at the cross."

## Sections (in order)

**Part 1: Setting the scene.** Who wrote it, to whom, what was happening, what came just before. Add one cultural or historical detail that unlocks the image (for example, wadis flooding in Matthew 7; Galatian false teachers in Galatians 1). Weave in one Ellen White reading here, described in your own words with book and chapter.

**Part 2: What the text actually says.** Three to five short observations from the text itself, often contrasts ("both builders heard the same words; both faced the same storm"). End with one original quote.

**Part 2b: Jesus in the passage.** A short section showing how the passage points to Christ. Look first at the passage's own context (the verses just before and after), then at how Jesus used the same image or lived the same truth, then at the cross and resurrection. Two or three short paragraphs, with references. End with an original quote about Jesus. For example, for 2 Corinthians 9:6: 8:9 ("though he was rich, yet for your sake he became poor"), John 12:24 (the kernel of wheat) and 9:15 ("his indescribable gift"). The quote: "Jesus didn't just teach sowing. He was the seed."

**Part 3: Everyday realities.** Five or six numbered scenarios. Each one:

```
**1. <Scenario title>**
<Two or three sentences describing a real, relatable situation.>
*Lesson:* <what Scripture says about it>
*Insight:* <a deeper, less obvious observation; where it fits naturally, what Jesus wants for us here or how He helps>
> **"<Original one-line quote>"**
```

Good scenario areas: the workplace, family expectations, marriage and parenting, money and shortcuts, social media, friendships and peer pressure, church service and motives, leadership, crisis and suffering, the private devotional life. Pick the ones the passage naturally speaks to. You may include one Bible character as a case study (for example Aaron, Saul, Pilate, Peter, Daniel), with an Ellen White chapter reference.

**Part 4: The roots.** Why we fall into this: three or four roots (fear, pride, impatience, insecurity, self-deception…) with a Scripture reference each, then one deep insight.

**Part 5: The balance.** Guard against legalism or harshness. Show how grace and obedience fit together, with one or two cross-references. Don't overcorrect: if the passage itself makes a promise or gives a warning, keep it, and explain how to hold it rightly rather than denying it. End with an original quote.

**Part 6: A simple self-check.** Three short questions the reader can ask before a decision.

**Part 7: This week's challenge.** Seven small, concrete daily actions (days may be grouped).

**Part 8: Ellen White anchor.** One verified direct quote with book and page, then a one-line takeaway.

**Summary quotes from this study.** Five of the strongest original quotes, bolded. Then: *(Bolded quotes are new ones written for this study, not Ellen White's.)*

**Closing prayer.** Three or four lines in italics, ending "Amen." It should name what Jesus has done and ask for His help to live it, not only for our own effort.

**Review notes.** Three to six short bullets from the theology review (see `theology-review.md`): what was checked and anything still unverified.

Close the reply with a one-line offer to make the WhatsApp caption and Instagram carousel.

## Writing the original quotes

- One line, under about 12 words where possible.
- Use contrast, parallelism or reversal: "Storms don't create cracks. They reveal them." / "Hearing makes you informed. Doing makes you founded."
- Practical, not abstract. It should be something a person can repeat at work on Monday.
- Theologically sound: grace first, obedience as response; no prosperity-gospel promises, but don't contradict a promise the passage itself makes.
- At least one of the five summary quotes should be about Jesus, not only about us.
- Never attribute an original quote to Ellen White or anyone else.
