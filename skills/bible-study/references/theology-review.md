# Theology review checklist

Run this on every study, every set of slide copy and every set of captions **before** delivering. Read the draft slowly and check each item. Fix anything that fails, then summarise the review in the study's **Review notes**.

The goal of every Abide study is to point to Jesus, His will for us, and what that means in everyday life. Most errors come from losing that centre, or from overcorrecting while trying to be practical.

## 1. Christ at the centre

- [ ] **Is Jesus in the big idea**, or at least in the opening and closing? A study that only mentions Him in the prayer fails.
- [ ] **Did you use the passage's own pointers to Christ?** Read the verses around the passage: Paul, Peter and John usually name Christ within a few verses of any command (for example 2 Corinthians 8:9 and 9:15 around 9:6; Ephesians 5:25 around 5:22–33).
- [ ] **Is there a Part 2b, "Jesus in the passage"**, showing how He fulfils, embodies or makes possible what the passage teaches?
- [ ] **Do the scenarios ask what Jesus wants for us here**, not only what we should do?
- [ ] **Does at least one summary quote and one slide (cover or close) point to Jesus?**
- [ ] **Does the closing prayer name what He has done** and ask for His help, not only for our effort?

## 2. Grace and response

- [ ] **Grace first.** Obedience, giving, forgiving and serving are presented as responses to grace and the fruit of the Spirit, not as ways to earn favour or blessing.
- [ ] **Where Scripture calls a virtue "grace", say so.** For example, Paul calls the Macedonians' giving "the grace that God has given" (2 Corinthians 8:1).
- [ ] **No moralism.** The study is not just "try harder". It shows where the power to change comes from (John 15:5; Philippians 2:13).
- [ ] **No legalism or shaming language** ("if you don't give, you are…"). Warnings are given with hope.

## 3. Don't overcorrect

- [ ] **Keep the passage's own promises and warnings.** If the text promises a harvest, a reward or a consequence, don't write a line that denies it (for example "We don't give to be blessed" contradicts 2 Corinthians 9:6). Explain how to hold the promise rightly instead.
- [ ] **No prosperity gospel.** Material return is never guaranteed. Frame God's promises as sufficiency for good works, spiritual fruit and eternal reward, as the passage does (2 Corinthians 9:8–11; Galatians 6:8).
- [ ] **No karma language.** Avoid presenting a principle as an impersonal "law of the universe". Root it in the character and faithfulness of God.

## 4. Faithful to the text

- [ ] **Is every verse used in its context?** Check each cross-reference: does it actually say what you're using it for? (For example, Galatians 6:7–8 is about sowing to the flesh or to the Spirit, not a general life-hack.)
- [ ] **Is every Bible quotation exact** in the version named (NIV by default)? Paraphrase without quotation marks if unsure.
- [ ] **Did you keep related doctrines distinct?** Don't conflate tithe with freewill offerings, Sabbath with Sunday worship, justification with sanctification, law with legalism, and so on. When a passage is about one, don't use it to teach the other without saying so.
- [ ] **Are word studies accurate?** Only cite Greek or Hebrew meanings you are sure of, such as "generously" being literally "with blessings" (*ep' eulogiais*) in 2 Corinthians 9:6.

## 5. Historical and factual accuracy

- [ ] **No invented background.** Only state historical details the text or well-established scholarship supports. If you're inferring, say "probably" or "possibly". (For example, the Jerusalem believers were "poor" according to Romans 15:26; the famine of Acts 11:28 was about ten years earlier.)
- [ ] **Names, places, dates and chapter references** are correct.
- [ ] **No cultural assumptions.** Don't assume one country, currency or culture unless the user has set it. Use neutral examples.

## 6. Ellen G. White

- [ ] **Every direct quote was either on the safe list in `egw-sources.md` or checked against the source** this session. If not, change it to a paraphrase with book and chapter.
- [ ] **Paraphrases were not presented as her words,** and they reflect what the chapter actually says. If you couldn't read the chapter, say so in the Review notes.
- [ ] **The anchor quote fits the theme.** Prefer one that points to Christ over a general character quote.
- [ ] **No original quote is attributed to her.**

## 7. Original quotes

- [ ] **Each one is true without its context.** Quotes are shared alone on slides and in WhatsApp, so read each one as a stranger would. Does it overstate, deny a promise or imply salvation by works?
- [ ] **No "automatic" language** that removes God's grace or human choice ("your hands open by themselves").
- [ ] **Short, memorable and repeatable**, in line with `study-format.md`.

## 8. Consistency across the pack

- [ ] **Slides, captions and study all say the same thing.** A correction in the study must also be made on the slides and in the captions.
- [ ] **Each slide stands alone** and is theologically sound without the slides around it.
- [ ] **The Friday challenge matches the study's challenge,** and the prayer matches the study's prayer.

## Review notes format

End the study with:

```
## Review notes
- Christ-centred: <how the study points to Jesus, in one line>
- Checked: <for example: cross-references in context, NIV wording, tithe vs offering kept distinct>
- Corrected in drafting: <any significant change, or "none">
- Unverified: <for example: EGW paraphrase of AA ch. 32 not checked against the text>, or "none"
```
